"You can't build muscle on a vegetarian diet." 

If you are an Indian lifter, you've probably heard this myth a hundred times. The truth is, while a pure vegetarian (no meat, no eggs) diet requires a bit more planning, it is absolutely possible to build an incredible physique on it.

Here is the blueprint for building muscle on a traditional Indian vegetarian diet.

## The Protein Math
To build muscle optimally, you need roughly 1.6g to 2.2g of protein per kilogram of body weight. For a 70kg lifter, that means aiming for about 110g to 150g of protein daily. 

If you can't rely on chicken or eggs, you need to turn to dairy, soy, and whey.

## The Vegetarian Protein Pillars

### 1. Soya Chunks (Nutrela)
- **Macros:** ~52g protein per 100g.
- **Why it's great:** It is the cheapest, most protein-dense vegetarian food available.
- **How to eat it:** Soak them, squeeze out the water, and cook them like a sabzi or mince them into a *soya bhurji*.

### 2. Paneer (Cottage Cheese)
- **Macros:** ~18g protein per 100g.
- **Why it's great:** Extremely versatile, high in calories (great for bulking), and packed with slow-digesting casein protein.
- **How to eat it:** Paneer tikka, raw paneer in salads, or classic palak paneer. (Just watch the oil!).

### 3. Whey Protein Isolate
- **Macros:** ~24g protein per scoop.
- **Why it's great:** Getting 130g of protein purely from whole veg foods can leave you feeling bloated due to the sheer volume of food. 1-2 scoops of high-quality whey isolate makes hitting your goals effortless.

### 4. Lentils & Legumes (Dal, Chana, Rajma)
- **Macros:** ~14g-18g protein per cup (cooked).
- **Why it's great:** Excellent sources of complex carbs and fiber.
- **The Catch:** They are not "complete" proteins on their own (lacking certain amino acids). However, eating them with rice or roti completes the amino acid profile.
